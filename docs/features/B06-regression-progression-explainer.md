# Regression / Progression Explainer

**Competitor verdict:** Partial — regression content is common, but few reframe the 4-month change as a permanent progression or flag that 12/18-month "regressions" are weakly supported.

## Problem / user need
"Sleep regression" is a parenting term, not a medical diagnosis, and the framing scares parents into thinking their baby is broken or backsliding. Parents need de-mystification: what actually changes, whether it's temporary, and reassurance that night wakings in year one are normal.

## What users actually say
Parents panic at the "4-month regression," search endlessly, and fear something is wrong. The brand promise is reassure, don't grade, and never imply a baby is "broken." Interpretation beats alarm.

## Competitor comparison
Most apps and blogs list "regressions" by age, often reinforcing the anxiety. Reframing 4 months as a one-way progression and being honest that later "regressions" are weakly supported is a differentiated, calming stance.

## Our approach (spec)
A short explainer, per age, that trades panic for understanding:
- **4-month change = permanent progression.** The brain's sleep system matures into adult-style stages, with cycles of about an hour (about 62 min at 3 months) and brief arousals between them; settling back between cycles is the skill that develops. The brain change is one-way — not a blip. Frame as growth. Be honest that population studies have not shown a 4-month rise in night waking (not that they ruled one out); some parents notice a change, some don't. No stated duration.
- **8–10 months:** starting to crawl (and early pulling to stand) is linked to a temporary rise in night waking (small studies). Separation anxiety as a cause of waking is untested, and attachment type did not predict waking — never state it as the cause. No stated duration.
- **12 / 18 / 24 months:** commonly cited but **weakly supported** as universal events; large cohorts show waking declining overall, with no setback visible at the ages they sampled (measurements months apart, so a brief rise in between is not ruled out). When sleep does change, point to specific causes with their own evidence level: walking onset (linked, small study), illness (linked to waking), teething (small and brief), and nap changes (practice convention, no study). Many experts prefer "progression."
- Reassurance throughout: night wakings are normal and near-universal in year one.

Each claim carries a **Tier badge + citation** (B01); T1 for the 4-month biology and the 8–10 month motor / attachment findings, T3 for the disputed later ones.

## Scope — MVP
Static explainer covering 4-month (progression framing), 8–10 month, and the honest 12/18-month caveat, plus the "night wakings are normal" reassurance. Tier badges + citations.

## Scope — later
Age-aware surfacing near relevant ages; link into the troubleshooter (B05); "what helps" range-based tips per phase; distinguish regression vs. nap transition (A06).

## Edge cases & gotchas
- Don't promise a fixed timeline or that every baby regresses. No source supports the popular "2–6 weeks".
- Don't state separation anxiety as the cause of 8–10 month waking.
- Avoid implying the baby is broken or that parents caused it.
- Keep the 12/18-month honesty from reading as dismissive of a struggling parent.

## Evidence & citations
Sourced claim by claim in research 10 (`docs/research/10-sleep-regressions-sourcing.md`), which supersedes research 02 §§6–7 where they differ.
- 4-month: brain maturation (`grigg-damberger-2007`), cycle length about an hour (`hammad-2026`), no population rise in waking (`scher-1991`, `gilchrist-2025-frontiers`), self-settling (`henderson-2010`), parent-noticed change (`sleepfoundation-4mo-regression`, T2) — Tier 1.
- 8–10 months: crawling and pulling to stand linked to waking (`scher-2015`, `scher-cohen-2005`, `atun-einy-2016`); separation anxiety → waking untested, attachment type did not predict waking (`scher-2001`, `weinraub-2012`) — Tier 1.
- 12/18/24-month weakly supported (`huckleberry-regression-myth`, Tier 3); waking declines overall, no setback visible at the ages sampled (`weinraub-2012`, `hysing-2014`); walking onset (`demasi-2023`), teething small and brief (`macknin-2000`), illness (`weinraub-2012`); nap changes are convention.
- Night wakings normal in year one (research 02 §3).
