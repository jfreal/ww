# 02 — Sleep Foundations & Nap Transitions

**Scope:** Newborn to ~15 months (with the 2→1 transition running to ~18 mo).
**Purpose:** The scheduling logic, transition rules, and safety/credibility anchors the app needs beyond raw wake-window numbers.
**Last researched:** July 2026. Evidence tiers as defined in `01-wake-window-science.md`.

---

## 1. Nap transitions: 4 → 3 → 2 → 1 (Tier 3, broadly consistent)

No controlled trial fixes a "correct" age to drop a nap; these are practitioner conventions built on wake-window maturation. Transitions are **gradual** — children commonly **alternate** old and new schedules for 1–several weeks.

| Transition | Typical age | Resulting schedule | Key readiness signs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **4 → 3 naps** | ~4–6 months | 3 naps; last is a short "catnap" | Fighting/shortening the 4th nap; windows lengthening; bedtime creeping late |
| **3 → 2 naps** | ~7–9 months (often 8–9) | Morning + afternoon | Resisting/dropping the 3rd nap; can handle ~2.5–3.5 h windows |
| **2 → 1 nap** | ~14–18 months (commonly 14–15) | Single midday nap ~1.5–3 h | Fighting a nap, short naps, or skipping a nap **≥4×/week for 1–2 weeks**; nap/bedtime resistance; early waking; split nights |
| **1 → 0 naps** | ~3–5 years | No nap; earlier bedtime | Consistent nap refusal without daytime deterioration |

**Distinguishing a real transition from a temporary regression:** nap resistance **+** short/skipped naps **+** early waking **+** split nights, occurring **together, consistently, at an age-appropriate window.**

**Technique:** lengthen wake windows **~15 min at a time** rather than dropping a nap abruptly, to avoid overtiredness.

**2→1 mechanics (consultant):** shift the morning nap ~15 min later every few days until it's ~5 h after wake / no earlier than ~11:00 AM, then phase out the second nap. Once fully on one nap, the child handles ~5 h of wake on each side; an early "bridge" bedtime prevents overtiredness during the switch.

Sources: [Huckleberry — Nap transitions](https://huckleberrycare.com/blog/nap-transitions-when-they-occur-and-how-to-handle-them) · [Huckleberry — 4-to-3](https://huckleberrycare.com/blog/4-to-3-nap-transition) · [Huckleberry — 2-to-1](https://huckleberrycare.com/blog/2-to-1-nap-transition) · [Taking Cara Babies — 2 naps to 1](https://www.takingcarababies.com/blogs/naps/transitioning-from-2-naps-to-1) · [Little Ones — Nap transitions](https://www.littleones.co/blogs/our-blog/nap-transitions-how-and-when-your-baby-will-drop-their-naps)

## 2. Nap length norms & consolidation (Tier 2/3)

- **Newborns (<3 mo):** 30–45 min catnaps are normal and expected; highly variable.
- **~12–16 weeks:** daytime sleep begins to **consolidate**; longer naps emerge. Chronic catnapping often resolves ~5–6 months as babies learn to link sleep cycles.
- **"45-minute intruder":** waking ~30–45 min into a nap (one sleep cycle) because the baby can't yet bridge to the next cycle. Most common ~4–5 months and around schedule shifts. A "full" restorative nap is generally ≥1–1.5 h.
- **Nap & memory (peer-reviewed):** infant naps support memory/motor-skill consolidation; very short naps are less restorative — an argument for protecting nap length.

## 3. Night sleep, wakings & "sleeping through the night" (Tier 1/2)

- **Night wakings are normal and near-universal in year one.** Brief arousals occur at the end of each sleep cycle; frequent waking ≠ sleep deprivation. **Design note: never imply a baby is "broken" for not sleeping through by 6–12 months.**
- **"Sleeping through the night" usually means ~6 consecutive hours**, not a full 10–12 h. Many babies become *physiologically capable* of longer stretches ~4–6 months (when night feeds are typically no longer nutritionally required — confirm with pediatrician), but **~25–50% of babies still wake overnight at 6 months.** If defined as 6–8 h, the more reliable window is ~8–9 months.
- **Bedtime timing:** once on a stable 2- or 1-nap schedule, most do best with bedtime **~7:00–8:00 PM**; on poor-nap days, an **earlier** bedtime limits overtiredness.
- **False start** = waking 30–45 min after bedtime (often under- or over-tired at bedtime). **Split night** = a long (2–3 h) wakeful mid-night period, usually from **too much day sleep / low sleep pressure** (undertired). Fixes target wake windows, total day sleep, and bedtime timing.

Sources: [Little Ones — Night waking by age](https://www.littleones.co/blogs/our-blog/night-waking-causes-and-expectations-by-age) · [Stanford Children's — Infant Sleep](https://www.stanfordchildrens.org/en/topic/default?id=infant-sleep-90-P02237) · [Little Ones — STTN](https://www.littleones.co/blogs/our-blog/sleeping-through-the-night) · [Pampers — sleep through the night](https://www.pampers.com/en-us/baby/sleep/article/when-do-babies-sleep-through-the-night) · [Taking Cara Babies — false starts](https://www.takingcarababies.com/blogs/newborn/my-baby-wont-stay-asleep-lets-talk-false-start-bedtimes) · [Cozy Baby Sleep — split nights](https://www.cozybabysleep.com/blog/split-nights) · [Baby Sleep Science — interpreting night wakings](https://www.babysleepscience.com/single-post/2014/11/05/interpreting-night-wakings)

## 4. Early morning waking (Tier 3)

Common causes and fixes cited consistently:

- **Overtired / too-late bedtime** → paradoxically causes early rising (cortisol). Fix: earlier bedtime, protect naps.
- **Too much day sleep / undertired** → not enough sleep pressure for the last stretch. Fix: trim naps or extend windows.
- **Light** — lightest sleep is ~2–7 AM; small light leaks wake them. Fix: **blackout shades**.
- **Temperature** (often too cold), hunger, and **lacking independent settling skills.**
- Treat wake-ups before **~6:00 AM** as night; give changes **≥1–2 weeks** to evaluate.

Sources: [Taking Cara Babies — early morning wakings](https://www.takingcarababies.com/blogs/sleep-basics/early-morning-wakings-why-is-my-baby-waking-up-early) · [Little Ones — early waking](https://www.littleones.co/blogs/our-blog/up-with-the-larks-tackling-early-waking) · [Huckleberry — early risers](https://huckleberrycare.com/blog/10-hidden-reasons-your-child-keeps-waking-too-early) · [Cozy Baby Sleep](https://www.cozybabysleep.com/blog/baby-waking-too-early-fix-5am-wakeups)

## 5. Sleep associations, "drowsy but awake" & self-settling (Tier 2 — with caveats)

- After the first few months, falling asleep involves **learned associations.** If a baby only ever falls asleep fed/rocked/held, they may need the same condition to resume sleep at each cycle transition.
- **Self-soothing — longitudinal evidence (Burnham/Anders cohort):** infants placed in the crib **awake** at the start of night, and who used a sleep aid, were more likely to self-soothe; predictors at 12 months included longer parental response times at 3 months.
- **"Drowsy but awake" — honest caveat:** the specific instruction has **not been rigorously tested on its own**; it's studied only bundled inside broader sleep-training programs, in babies ~6 mo+. Present it as a low-cost technique, **not** an evidence-backed guarantee. The real goal is **independent sleep onset**, not a particular drowsiness state.

Sources: [The Conversation (academic) — Does "drowsy but awake" work?](https://theconversation.com/why-are-parents-told-to-put-their-baby-to-bed-drowsy-but-awake-does-it-work-183368) · [Burnham/Anders — self-soothing, PMC1201414](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1201414/) · [Self-soothing birth to one year, PMC1201415](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1201415/) · [Precious Little Sleep](https://www.preciouslittlesleep.com/what-you-need-to-know-about-sleeping-through-the-night-part-2/) · [Mommywise critique](https://mommywise.com/blog/worst-sleep-training-tip-drowsy-but-awake/)

## 6. The "4-month regression" is a permanent progression (Tier 1/2 biology)

- Newborns lack a mature circadian rhythm and produce little melatonin (production begins ~8–12 weeks). Day–night confusion typically resolves ~6–8 weeks.
- Around **3–5 months**, newborn sleep reorganizes into cyclical NREM/REM staging with cycles of **about an hour** (about 62 min at 3 months by actigraphy, lengthening about 10 min by 12 months — Hammad 2026) and brief arousals between cycles. Settling back to sleep between cycles is the skill that develops from here. The older "~45–60 min" figure has no source (doc 10).
- The **brain** change is **one-way, not a temporary blip** (Grigg-Damberger 2007). **Frame it as a progression in-app.** No source supports a fixed duration for any sleep disruption around it; do not state one (doc 10, F12).
- Population studies have **not shown** a rise in night waking at 4 months: regular waking was 46% at 3 months and 39% at 6 months (Scher 1991, measured 3 months apart, so a brief rise between is not ruled out), and actigraphy waking peaked about 12 weeks, then fell (Gilchrist 2025). Some parents do notice a change; say so as parent report, not as a measured event (doc 10, F9–F10).

Sources: [Hammad 2026 — infant sleep cycles by actigraphy](https://doi.org/10.1093/sleep/zsag161) · [Grigg-Damberger 2007 — AASM infant sleep scoring](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17557427/) · [Scher 1991 — night waking in the first year](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2214.1991.tb00699.x) · [Gilchrist 2025 — maturation of infant sleep, first 6 months](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2025.1581325/full) · [Sleep Foundation — 4-month regression](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/baby-sleep/4-month-sleep-regression) · [Little Ones — 4-month](https://www.littleones.co/blogs/our-blog/the-4-month-sleep-regression) · [Nanit — 4-month schedule](https://www.nanit.com/blogs/baby-sleep-schedule/4-month-sleep-schedule-your-complete-guide-to-navigating-the-sleep-regression) · [Little Ones — Melatonin & babies](https://www.littleones.co/blogs/our-blog/melatonin-and-babies)

## 7. Sleep regressions — real vs. myth (Tier 2/3)

- **Only the 4-month change has a clear, universal biological driver** — the EEG maturation. A behavioural regression at 4 months is not documented at population level (Scher 1991, Gilchrist 2025). Others are **developmental windows**, not fixed events, and not every baby experiences them. "Sleep regression" is a **parenting term, not a medical diagnosis.**
- **8–10 months:** starting to crawl is **linked to** more night waking for a while (Scher & Cohen 2005; Scher 2015; small samples). Separation protest peaks around 9–13 months (Kearsley 1975), but no study has tested whether it causes night waking; the baby's attachment *classification* did not predict waking (Scher 2001; Weinraub 2012). Do not state that separation anxiety drives the waking. No source supports a duration (doc 10, E1–E10).
- **12/18/24-month "regressions"** are commonly cited but **weakly supported** as universal — large cohorts show waking declining overall from 6 to 24 months, with no setback visible at the ages measured (Weinraub 2012; Hysing 2014; measurements months apart, so a brief rise is not ruled out); they cluster around milestones, nap transitions, teething, illness, and language/motor bursts. Many experts prefer "progression."

Sources: [Huckleberry — 12-month: myth or real?](https://huckleberrycare.com/blog/12-month-sleep-regression) · [Taking Cara Babies — 8–10 mo](https://www.takingcarababies.com/blogs/regressions/8-10-month-sleep-regression) · [Batelle — 8-month](https://batelle.com/sleep-regressions/the-8-month-sleep-regression/) · [Little Ones — separation anxiety](https://www.littleones.co/blogs/our-blog/separation-anxiety-in-babies-toddlers) · [Pinky McKay — myth of regressions](https://pinkymckay.com/the-myth-of-baby-sleep-regressions-whats-really-happening-to-your-babys-sleep/)

## 8. Safe sleep — AAP 2022 policy (TIER 1, NON-NEGOTIABLE)

A credible sleep app **must** surface these. ~3,500 U.S. infants die suddenly during sleep annually (SIDS + accidental suffocation/strangulation). All apply to **age <1 year** unless noted.

- **Back to sleep, every sleep, until age 1** — every nap and night, every caregiver. Back sleeping does **not** increase choking risk.
- **Firm, flat, non-inclined surface** (incline <10°): CPSC-compliant crib/bassinet/play yard; fitted sheet only. Inclined sleepers, in-bed sleepers, nests/pods, loungers (Boppy, DockATot) are **not** for sleep.
- **No bed-sharing**, under any circumstances. Sofa/armchair co-sleeping raises risk up to **~67×**. Highest risk if baby <4 months, preterm/low birth weight, or adult used alcohol/cannabis/sedating meds or smokes.
- **Room-share (separate surface) for ≥6 months** — can reduce SIDS risk up to **~50%**.
- **Keep soft objects & loose bedding out** — no pillows, blankets, quilts, bumpers, stuffed toys, weighted blankets/swaddles. Use a wearable blanket; dress ~one layer more than an adult.
- **Avoid overheating**; no hat indoors after hospital.
- **Other risk reducers:** breastfeeding/human milk; **pacifier** at sleep (once breastfeeding established); no smoking/nicotine/vaping; keep well-child visits & vaccinations; supervised awake **tummy time.**
- **Swaddling is allowed but does not reduce SIDS** — always place swaddled babies on the back and **stop swaddling at first signs of rolling (~3–4 months).**
- **Home cardiorespiratory/consumer O₂/HR monitors are not recommended** to reduce SIDS.

Sources: [AAP 2022 Policy — Sleep-Related Infant Deaths, Pediatrics](https://publications.aap.org/pediatrics/article/150/1/e2022057990/188304/Sleep-Related-Infant-Deaths-Updated-2022) · [AAP 2022 Technical Report](https://publications.aap.org/pediatrics/article/150/1/e2022057991/188305/Evidence-Base-for-2022-Updated-Recommendations-for) · [HealthyChildren.org — Safe Sleep parent guide](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/ages-stages/baby/sleep/Pages/a-parents-guide-to-safe-sleep.aspx) · [Cribs for Kids — AAP 2022 summary](https://cribsforkids.org/aap-recommendations-2022/)

## 9. Sleep training — methods, efficacy & safety (Tier 1)

**Methods:** unmodified extinction ("cry it out"); graduated extinction (Ferber, timed check-ins); bedtime fading / positive routines; scheduled awakenings; chair method / camping out; parent education (prevention).

**Efficacy (Tier 1):** AASM-commissioned review (Mindell et al., 2006) of **52 studies** found **94% reported behavioral interventions efficacious**, with **>80% of treated children showing clinically significant, durable (3–6 mo) improvement.**

**Bedtime routine (Tier 1 RCT):** Mindell's RCT (405 pairs) showed a consistent 3-step nightly routine significantly reduced sleep-onset latency and night wakings and improved maternal mood — **dose-dependent** (more nights → better outcomes).

**Long-term safety (Tier 1):** Price et al. (2012), 5-year follow-up of a randomized trial (326 children), found **no differences vs. controls** on emotional/behavioral problems, sleep, stress (cortisol), attachment, or parent mental health — **no marked long-lasting effects, good or bad.**

**The cortisol concern, in context:** the widely-shared Middlemiss (2012) study had **only 25 infants, no control group, no baseline, 2 days of data** — too weak to support harm claims. Larger controlled evidence shows no lasting stress/attachment harm.

**Age readiness:** not appropriate under ~4 months; the evidence base is in infants **~4–6 months+.** Frame supportively and inclusively (many parents feel judged here — the app's brand is "reassure, don't grade").

Sources: [Mindell et al. 2006, PubMed 17068979](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17068979/) · [AASM review PDF](https://aasm.org/resources/practiceparameters/review_nightwakingschildren.pdf) · [Mindell RCT — bedtime routines, PMC4402657](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4402657/) · [Price et al. 2012, Pediatrics](https://publications.aap.org/pediatrics/article/130/4/643/30241/Five-Year-Follow-up-of-Harms-and-Benefits-of) · [Betteroo — studies audited](https://betteroo.ai/guides/does-sleep-training-work-what-5-major-studies-actually-show/) · [Jolly Good Sleep — Middlemiss explained](https://www.jollygoodsleep.com/post/the-sleep-training-cortisol-study-what-the-middlemiss-research-actually-showed)

## 10. Bedtime routines & healthy-sleep tips (Tier 1 / AAP)

- **Consistent routine matters** (bath → book → bed) — improves sleep onset/continuity and maternal mood, dose-dependently. Start in infancy.
- **AAP tips:** regular daily schedule; **no solids before ~6 months to "help sleep"** (it doesn't); no bottle of milk/juice in bed; screens out of the bedroom and off ≥60 min before bed; dim lights, cool room; avoid over-scheduling.
- **Scheduling logic:** wake windows drive nap timing before a "by-the-clock" schedule (feasible ~6+ months); protect total day sleep to balance sleep pressure and prevent both early waking and split nights. **Preterm: use adjusted age.**

Sources: [HealthyChildren.org — Healthy Sleep tips](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/healthy-living/sleep/Pages/healthy-sleep-habits-how-many-hours-does-your-child-need.aspx) · [Mindell RCT, PMC4402657](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4402657/) · [Huckleberry — sleep schedule by age](https://huckleberrycare.com/blog/baby-sleep-schedule-by-age-nap-and-sleep-chart)
