## FAQ by theme

### 1. Wake-window mechanics (the definitional questions)

- **Q: What is a wake window?** — A: Time awake between one sleep and the next, used to time naps before overtiredness. *(TCB wake-windows; Cleveland Clinic; The Bump)*
- **Q: ★ Does the window start when baby's eyes open?** — A: Yes — awake time starts at wake-up (TCB frames it "out of the crib to back in the crib"); it does not restart after a feed. *(Happiest Baby; TCB; The Bump)*
- **Q: ★ Does feeding count toward the window?** — A: Yes — feeding, changes, and play all count. *(TCB; The Bump)*
- **Q: Does time spent falling asleep count?** — A: No — the window ends at lay-down, not sleep onset; 5–20 min settling (20–30 for toddlers) is normal. *(TCB; The Bump)*
- **Q: Are windows rules or guidelines?** — A: Guidelines — combine with cues; flex for transitions, teething, illness, travel. *(Huckleberry; Owlet; The Bump)*
- **Q: What if the window runs too long?** — A: Overtiredness — fights sleep, fragmented naps. *(Little Ones; TCB overtired)*
- **Q: What if it's too short?** — A: Undertiredness — hard settling, short naps, false starts, night waking; extend awake time. *(Little Ones; Huckleberry false-starts)*
- **Q: What are the tired signs?** — A: Early: yawning, eye-rubbing, staring, slowing down. Late: fussing, arched back, frantic crying (already overtired). *(Cleveland Clinic; Happiest Baby)*
- **Q: Overtired vs undertired — how do I tell?** — A: Overtired = distressed, rigid, hard to soothe, frequent waking. Undertired = content/playful in crib, just takes ages to fall asleep. *(TCB overtired; Baby Sleep Site)*
- **Q: Should the last window before bed be the longest?** — A: Yes — windows typically lengthen across the day. *(TCB nap-schedules)*

### 2. Schedules vs cues; when a "real" schedule emerges

- **Q: ★ Wake windows, sleepy cues, or the clock?** — A: Under ~6m windows+cues; ~6m+ shift toward clock (works best on 2 naps); blend during transitions. *(Huckleberry windows-vs-clock)*
- **Q: Are sleepy cues reliable?** — A: Not fully — boredom mimics tiredness; use cues with timing. *(Little Ones)*
- **Q: When do babies get predictable?** — A: Consolidation starts ~3–4m as circadian rhythm matures; fixed schedules rarely before 6m. *(Huckleberry chart; Sleep Foundation newborn)*
- **Q: Do newborns have a schedule at all?** — A: No — first ~2 months are scattered 1–4h naps driven by feeding. *(Sleep Foundation newborn)*
- **Q: When can I stop tracking windows entirely?** — A: Once consistently on one nap and ≥ ~18m, go by the clock. *(TCB nap-schedules)*

### 3. Nap counts, lengths, totals

- **Q: ★ How many naps at each age?** — A: 0–3m: 4–6+ irregular; 4–6m: 3–4; 6–9m: 3→2; 9–14m: 2; 14–18m+: 1. *(Baby Sleep Site chart; Huckleberry; Mayo)*
- **Q: How long should each nap be?** — A: Newborn 10min–4h is normal; after ~5–6m aim ~1–2h, last nap short. *(Baby Sleep Site; Mayo)*
- **Q: How much total day sleep?** — A: ~4–5h newborn → 3–4h at 3–6m → 2–3h 6–18m → 1.5–2.5h on one nap. *(Baby Sleep Site; Nested Bean)*
- **Q: ★ Can baby nap too much? Should I wake a sleeping baby?** — A: Yes — cap a single nap at ~2h on multi-nap schedules (~3h on one nap) to protect feeds and night sleep. *(TCB should-you-wake; Huckleberry too-much-day-sleep)* *(Counterpoint worth a tier note: Reynaud 2025 actigraphy — preschoolers 2–5, adjacent-age evidence — found naps trade only ~13.6 min/h against night sleep and end-time matters more than duration; see doc 06.)*
- **Q: Same wake time every morning?** — A: Anchor the day by ~8:00am or 12–12.5h after bedtime. *(TCB should-you-wake)*
- **Q: Ideal nap environment?** — A: Dark (blackout), continuous white noise, cool. *(Mayo; Peaceful Sleeper)*

### 4. Short naps / the 30–45 minute nap

- **Q: ★ Why only 30–45 minutes?** — A: Waking at the end of one sleep cycle (~45–50 min) without linking to the next — sleep-onset associations, over/undertiredness, environment, hunger. *(TCB short-naps; PLS short-nap guide)*
- **Q: Are short naps normal?** — A: Yes under ~4–5m; consolidation follows. *(TCB; Baby Sleep Site)*
- **Q: What's the "45-minute intruder"?** — A: The cycle-boundary waking mid-nap. *(TCB)*
- **Q: Should I resettle after a short nap?** — A: Under 5m yes (hold/rock to finish the cycle); older: crib hour or training. *(TCB; Peaceful Sleeper)*
- **Q: What is "crib hour"?** — A: Keep baby in the crib the full hour from nap start (if calm) to teach cycle-linking. *(Peaceful Sleeper; PLS)* *(Heuristic — no direct evidence; mark T3 if adopted.)*
- **Q: Is a short last nap fine?** — A: Yes — the cat nap just bridges to bedtime. *(TCB; PLS)*
- **Q: How do I lengthen naps?** — A: Darkness, white noise, right-sized windows, full feeds, crib hour/resettling, consistency. *(Peaceful Sleeper; PLS)*

### 5. Fighting naps / nap strikes

- **Q: Why is baby suddenly fighting naps?** — A: Overtired, undertired, environment, or ready to drop a nap; leaps/teething cause temporary strikes. *(Baby Sleep Site fighting-naps)*
- **Q: What do I do during a strike?** — A: Find the cause, adjust windows, protect bedtime (earlier bedtime or bridging catnap), keep offering the nap. *(Baby Sleep Site; Huckleberry transitions)*

### 6. Contact naps, motion naps, naps on the go

- **Q: ★ Are contact naps bad? Am I building a bad habit?** — A: No — normal, bonding-positive; some babies later need help learning independent naps. *(TCB contact-naps; KellyMom)*
- **Q: Are contact naps safe?** — A: Only with an awake adult watching; adult falling asleep on couch/armchair with baby raises sleep-death risk up to 67×. *(TCB contact-naps; AAP safe-sleep)*
- **Q: How do I move from contact naps to crib naps?** — A: Environment, right windows, practice 1–2 independent naps/day awake-at-laydown; nights consolidate before naps. *(TCB)*
- **Q: Do stroller/car-seat naps count? Is motion sleep worse?** — A: They count; no strong proof it's less restorative; practical issues are shorter naps + motion dependence; AAP: move to a firm flat surface as soon as practical. *(Today's Parent; PLS; AAP safe-sleep)*
- **Q: Is nursing to sleep a bad habit?** — A: No — biologically normal; independent settling is a milestone reached when ready. *(KellyMom)*

### 7. False starts & early morning waking

- **Q: ★ What's a false start?** — A: Waking within ~30–60 min of bedtime; schedule- or association-driven. *(Huckleberry false-starts)*
- **Q: Why the 30–45-min post-bedtime wake?** — A: Over- or undertiredness, associations, discomfort; right-size windows, build independent settling. *(Huckleberry; The Bump)*
- **Q: What counts as an early morning waking?** — A: 4:00–6:00am; after 6:00 is morning. *(TCB early-waking)*
- **Q: ★ Why 5am, and how do I fix it?** — A: Sleep pressure and melatonin are lowest 4–6am — light leaks, day-sleep imbalance, or late bedtime tip baby awake; blackout, adjust day sleep, often earlier bedtime; hold each change ≥1 week. *(TCB early-waking)*

### 8. Night–day balance & newborn day/night confusion

- **Q: Will long naps ruin nights?** — A: Excess day sleep can — bedtime battles, night waking, early mornings. *(TCB; Huckleberry)*
- **Q: Newborn sleeps all day, parties all night?** — A: Day/night confusion from immature circadian clock; bright days, dark boring nights, wake from naps >~2h; resolves by ~8 weeks. *(Huckleberry day-night; Sleep Foundation newborn)*
- **Q: When will baby sleep through the night?** — A: Physiologically possible ~4–6m for many. *(Huckleberry; Sleep Foundation)*
- **Q: Do bad naps cause bad nights (and vice versa)?** — A: Yes, both directions. *(TCB short-naps; TCB overtired)*

### 9. Nap transitions & dropping the nap

- **Q: ★ When do the 4→3, 3→2, 2→1 transitions happen?** — A: ~4–6m; ~7–9m; ~14–18m (some 12m); nap→none between 3–5y. *(Huckleberry transitions; TCB nap-schedules)*
- **Q: Signs baby is ready to drop a nap?** — A: Nap refusal 3–4×/week for ~2 weeks, shortening naps, EMW, split nights — persistent over weeks, not days. *(Huckleberry 2-to-1; TCB)*
- **Q: How long does a transition take?** — A: Weeks; alternating 1-nap and 2-nap days is normal. *(Huckleberry)*
- **Q: Avoiding overtiredness during the transition?** — A: Earlier bedtime (even 6:00pm), extend windows in ~15-min steps, occasional rescue catnap. *(Huckleberry 2-to-1)*
- **Q: When does napping stop altogether?** — A: Most children between 3 and 5. *(Mayo; Huckleberry)*

### 10. Regressions

- **Q: ★ The 4-month regression — what and how long?** — A: Permanent sleep-cycle maturation at 3–4m; no sourced duration (the popular "2–6 weeks" is blog-only — see research/10 H11/F12); population studies have not shown a 4-month rise in night waking (research/10 F9); independent settling speeds recovery; naps go 30–45min too. *(Huckleberry 4-month; Sleep Foundation)*
- **Q: Is the 12-month regression real?** — A: No biological evidence — usually a schedule that needs adjusting. *(Huckleberry first-year)*

### 11. Daycare

- **Q: ★ Baby won't nap at daycare — help?** — A: Share home routine/windows, ask for white noise + quieter spot + consistent timing, send familiar items, allow adjustment time. *(Sleep Lady; TCB daycare)*
- **Q: Daycare schedule ≠ home schedule — problem?** — A: No — babies can run two schedules; compensate lost day sleep with an early (~6pm) bedtime. *(TCB daycare)*
- **Q: Car nap on the way home — does it count?** — A: <30 min: add 10–45 min to the wake window; longer: count as a nap and time bedtime accordingly. *(TCB daycare)*
- **Q: Naps at daycare but not at home?** — A: Routine/peer effect there; tighten windows, environment, routine at home; early bedtime on no-nap days. *(TCB daycare)*

### 12. Preemies / adjusted age

- **Q: ★ Adjusted or actual age for windows?** — A: Adjusted (actual minus weeks early) for all sleep expectations through ~2 years. *(Huckleberry preemie)*
- **Q: When will my preemie consolidate?** — A: Patterns ~3–4m adjusted; consolidated nights often ~6–8m adjusted. *(Huckleberry preemie)*

### 13. Twins & siblings

- **Q: Should twins nap together?** — A: Yes — sync; wake the second twin within 15–30 min of the first. *(Huckleberry twins)*
- **Q: Can twins share a crib for naps?** — A: No — AAP: one sleep surface each; room-sharing fine. *(Huckleberry twins; AAP)*
- **Q: Different sleep needs between twins?** — A: Keep roughly synced; give the sleepier twin longer naps or earlier bedtime. *(Huckleberry twins)*

### 14. Travel & daylight saving

- **Q: How do I handle DST?** — A: Gradual 10–15 min/day shift for a week, or stretch windows 5–15 min over the weekend, or jump to new time; up to two weeks to adjust; ignore for <4m. *(TCB DST; Huckleberry)*
- **Q: Home time or local time when traveling?** — A: Few days: home time. Longer: switch using daylight + routine; cap naps at 2h to protect bedtime. *(TCB travel)*

### 15. Safe sleep at naptime

- **Q: Same rules as night?** — A: Yes — back, firm flat surface, bare crib, every nap and night through age 1. *(AAP safe-sleep; Mayo)*
- **Q: Swing/car seat/carrier naps?** — A: Not routine sleep spaces; transfer to firm flat surface ASAP. *(AAP)*
- **Q: Nap in my room?** — A: Room-share (not bed-share) ≥ first 6 months; up to ~50% SIDS risk reduction. *(AAP)*

### 16. Sleep training & naps

- **Q: When can I nap-train?** — A: ≥5 months. *(TCB nap-training)*
- **Q: Are naps harder to train than nights?** — A: Yes — lower day sleep pressure/melatonin; takes longer. *(TCB)*
- **Q: Naps and nights together or nights first?** — A: Nights-first is common; simultaneous also works; nights consolidate first. *(TCB)*
- **Q: What's "drowsy but awake"?** — A: Down sleepy but conscious, to practice independent settling. *(Mayo; Cleveland Clinic)*
